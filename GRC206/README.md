## Overview

This repository contains my completed **GRC206 Security Awareness Program Design** for the fictional **Northbridge Logistics**, an organisation with 800 employees working across its head office, warehouses, remote sales teams, and IT administration.

The project presents a practical, measurable, and sustainable 12-month security awareness programme designed for a small security team and a limited first-year budget. It focuses on reducing human-driven risks while building a supportive, no-blame reporting culture.

> **Disclaimer:** Northbridge Logistics and the programme data are part of an academic scenario. All targets are proposed planning objectives, not existing organisational results.

## Key Risks Addressed

1. **Business Email Compromise and invoice fraud** targeting Finance and Procurement.
2. **Password reuse and weak credential hygiene**, increasing the risk of account takeover.
3. **Phishing and suspicious-link handling** across a dispersed, mixed-device workforce.

---

## Audience Segmentation

| Audience | Primary Training Need |
|---|---|
| Head Office, Finance, Procurement, HR, and Admin | BEC, invoice-fraud red flags, callback verification, and regulated-data handling |
| Warehouse Operations | Shared-terminal hygiene, social engineering, suspicious visitors, and badge reporting |
| Remote Sales | Smishing, secure remote access, public Wi-Fi, and lost-device reporting |
| IT and System Administrators | MFA, privileged-account hygiene, and basic incident triage |

---

## Programme Objectives

- Reduce simulated-phishing click rates to **below 10% within six months** and **below 5% within 12 months**.
- Increase suspicious-email reporting to **25% within six months** and **40% within 12 months**.
- Achieve **90% onboarding completion within 30 days** and **95% annual refresher completion by Month 9**.

---

## Delivery Approach

- Quarterly workshops and monthly micro-learning for office-based staff
- Shift-start toolbox talks, posters, and quick-reference cards for warehouse teams
- Mobile-first micro-learning and quarterly virtual sessions for remote sales
- Biannual technical briefings and targeted advisories for IT administrators
- Role-based onboarding within the first week of employment
- Quarterly phishing simulations for email-enabled staff
- Paper-based “spot the scam” exercises for warehouse staff without individual email access
- Friendly, immediate coaching after simulation clicks rather than punitive messaging

---

## Measurement and Governance

The programme tracks four core measures:

| Metric | Purpose | Review Cycle |
|---|---|---|
| Training completion rate | Measures programme reach | Monthly and quarterly |
| Phishing click rate | Measures risky behaviour | After each quarterly campaign |
| Phishing report rate | Measures positive reporting behaviour | Monthly and after each campaign |
| Fraud-attempt incidents | Measures business outcomes | Quarterly |

A one-page quarterly dashboard is provided to the executive sponsor and audience owners. Results are reviewed alongside limitations such as self-report bias, small audience samples, and the lagging nature of fraud data.

---

## Continuous Improvement

The programme includes:

- Quarterly reviews with named owners and due dates
- Anonymous learner feedback after training activities
- Content updates following new scams, incidents, near-misses, or process changes
- A monthly **Great Catch** recognition feature
- Supportive recognition for employees who report their own mistakes
- Annual leadership reporting and programme review

## Key Takeaway

An effective security awareness programme should be practical enough for the organisation to sustain. This design uses four focused metrics, audience-specific delivery methods, quarterly reviews, and low-cost tools so that a small security team can operate the programme throughout the full 12-month period.
