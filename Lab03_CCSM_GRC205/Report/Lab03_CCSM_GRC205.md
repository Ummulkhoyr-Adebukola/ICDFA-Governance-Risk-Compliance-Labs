## Executive Summary

This report presents a crisis communication and stakeholder-management response to a suspected cyber incident affecting a critical organisational file share. The disruption began at approximately 08:15 on a Monday. At the same time, an unverified screenshot circulated through staff chats, creating a risk that rumour, misinformation, or leaked material could shape the public narrative before the organisation established verified facts. Friday payroll was also potentially at risk because the affected file share might support payroll systems or data.

As Communications Lead to the Incident Commander, the objective was to establish a single credible source of truth during the first four hours. The response balanced speed with accuracy by separating confirmed facts, working assumptions, and unknowns; defining a controlled approval chain; prioritising stakeholders; preparing audience-specific messages; and using out-of-band communication channels because corporate email, chat, and directory services might be compromised.

The central communication principle was to issue a small, accurate message quickly and commit to a fixed time for the next update. The report deliberately avoids claiming that ransomware, a breach, data exfiltration, an attacker, or a regulatory reporting duty has been confirmed. It also avoids promising that payroll will run normally before the relevant systems, data, and bank cut-offs have been verified.

Key deliverables include a four-hour communication plan, stakeholder map, verification gates, escalation triggers, employee and customer statements, an executive briefing, a channel-resilience plan, and a critical reflection on communication under uncertainty.

## 1. Scenario and Communication Challenge

### 1.1 Confirmed Situation

- Several teams cannot access a critical shared file drive.
- The disruption began at approximately 08:15.
- Security is investigating the matter as a possible cyber incident.
- An unverified screenshot is circulating through staff chats and beyond.
- Friday payroll may depend on systems or data connected to the affected file share.

### 1.2 Unknowns

The following matters remain unconfirmed:

- Attacker identity
- Root cause
- Initial entry point
- Number of affected systems
- Whether data was accessed or exfiltrated
- Expected recovery time
- Whether a legal or regulatory notification duty has started
- Whether payroll can proceed safely and on time

### 1.3 Communication Risks

- Rumour may become the accepted narrative before verified information is released.
- Multiple spokespeople may issue inconsistent statements.
- Staff may spread the screenshot or contact people named in it.
- Attackers may exploit uncertainty through phishing or fraudulent payment requests.
- An unsupported payroll promise may create employee-relations and legal consequences.
- Corporate communication systems may be unavailable or compromised.
- Premature language such as “ransomware,” “breach,” or “attack” may create legal and reputational exposure.
- Delayed contact with banks, insurers, counsel, or regulators may affect response options.

## 3. Communication Objectives

The response aims to:

1. Establish one credible source of truth.
2. Prevent the unverified screenshot from controlling the narrative.
3. Give each stakeholder group the information and action it needs.
4. Protect legal privilege and regulatory decision-making.
5. Maintain employee trust without making unsupported payroll promises.
6. Reduce phishing, fraud, and information-leak risks.
7. Keep executives and the Board informed of decisions and exposure.
8. Maintain communication if corporate email and chat are unavailable.
9. Create an auditable record of messages, approvals, acknowledgements, and decisions.
10. Issue updates at fixed times, even when facts are unchanged.

## 4. Communication Governance

### 4.1 Approval Chain

Every message follows the same controlled process:

1. The Incident Commander confirms the operational facts.
2. Legal reviews the wording and disclosure risk.
3. The Incident Commander authorises release.
4. The Communications Lead distributes and records the message.

### 4.2 Disclosure Rules

- Only named spokespeople may communicate externally.
- All other staff refer questions to the Communications Lead.
- Use **“possible cyber incident”** until Security confirms otherwise.
- Do not state “ransomware,” “breach,” or “attack” as fact without evidence.
- If information is not in the approved fact log, it is not communicated.
- Describe unresolved matters as **under investigation**.
- Do not repeat or validate claims from the screenshot.
- State the next update time in every stakeholder message.
- Preserve every approved message and record its release time, owner, audience, and channel.

### 4.3 Fact-Log Structure

The incident fact log should distinguish:

| Category | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Confirmed fact | Supported by an authorised operational or technical source |
| Assumption | Used for planning but not yet verified |
| Unknown | Requires investigation before it can be communicated as fact |
| Decision | Approved action with a named owner and timestamp |
| Communication | Message, audience, channel, approval, and delivery status |

## 5. First Four-Hour Communication Plan

| Time | Action | Owner | Output |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 08:30 | Conduct a 15-minute incident-command alignment call using an out-of-band bridge | Incident Commander and Communications Lead | Fact log v1, named spokespeople, disclosure rules, approval chain, and checkpoints |
| 08:45 | Provide a verbal briefing to the CEO | Incident Commander | CEO understands confirmed facts, unknowns, and timing |
| 08:45-09:25 | Draft employee and customer messages, complete Legal review, and track screenshot spread | Communications Lead and Legal | Approved drafts and initial leak log |
| 09:30 | Issue employee holding statement by SMS | Communications Lead | Message released and acknowledgement tracking started |
| 09:30-10:30 | Contain the screenshot, preserve evidence, remove internal copies, and monitor external posts | Communications Lead and Security | Preserved copy and leak log |
| 10:45 | Update the fact log with the CISO | Incident Commander and CISO | Fact log v2 with updated confirmed and unknown lists |
| 11:00 | Issue executive and Board briefing | Communications Lead and Incident Commander | Decisions and approvals requested |
| 11:15-11:30 | Contact bank and payroll partners, then send approved customer and partner update | Finance Lead and Customer Lead | Calls logged and update issued |
| 12:00 | Review escalation triggers and the next communication draft | Incident Commander | Go or hold decision for 12:30 message |
| 12:30 | Issue second employee update | Communications Lead | First communication cycle closed |

### 5.1 Rationale for the Sequence

Alignment comes first because an early guess or a contradictory spokesperson can create more harm than a short delay. The CEO receives a verbal briefing before employees are contacted. Employees are then prioritised because the screenshot is spreading through internal chats and staff need immediate instructions. Customers, partners, banks, and other external stakeholders receive information after facts and wording have been checked.

## 6. Verification Gates

| Information | Verification Owner | Requirement Before Communication |
|---|---|---|
| Attacker identity | Security and external specialists | Verified and approved by the Incident Commander and Legal |
| Root cause and entry point | Forensics | Investigation confirms the finding |
| Number of affected systems | Security | Asset assessment is complete |
| Data exposure | Forensics and Legal | Evidence of access or exfiltration exists |
| Recovery time | IT and vendors | A tested restoration plan exists |
| Regulatory reporting duty | General Counsel | Legal assesses verified facts against applicable law |
| Payroll status | Payroll Lead and bank | Payroll systems are confirmed safe and the bank cut-off is known |

Until a gate is passed, the approved wording is **“under investigation”** or **“not yet confirmed.”**

## 8. Escalation Triggers

Any one of the following triggers escalation to the Board Chair, Legal-led external contact, and a formal decision meeting:

- Evidence that data left the organisation
- Evidence that the screenshot contains personal or confidential information
- Payroll cannot be confirmed safe to run by Wednesday at 12:00
- Contact or a demand is received from an attacker
- A media or regulator enquiry is received
- The screenshot becomes publicly visible
- The primary incident-command channel fails

Only the Incident Commander and Legal may engage with an attacker.

## 9. Employee Holding Statement

> **Subject: IT disruption: what we know and what we need from you**
>
> Since about 08:15 today, several teams have been unable to open the shared file drive. Our security team is investigating and is treating this as a possible cyber incident. We have not confirmed the cause or how many systems are affected, and we will not guess.
>
> You may have seen a screenshot circulating in staff chats. It has not been verified, and we are not treating it as accurate. Please do not forward, reply to, or discuss it, and do not contact anyone named in it. Send a copy to the security hotline on `[number]`, then delete it from group chats.
>
> Until further notice, please do not use the shared drive, avoid unexpected attachments and USB devices, do not attempt your own file recovery, and refer customer or media enquiries to `[Communications contact]`.
>
> Friday's payroll is a priority. We cannot yet confirm timing, and we will not promise what we cannot verify. We will tell you where it stands at 12:30, even if the answer is still unknown.
>
> **Next update:** 12:30 by SMS. If email is unavailable, call `[number]`.

### 9.1 Message Strengths

- Acknowledges the disruption without exaggerating it
- Addresses the screenshot directly
- Gives staff clear behaviour-based instructions
- Reduces phishing and evidence-destruction risk
- Treats payroll honestly
- Provides a fixed update time and fallback channel

## 12. Channel-Resilience Plan

Corporate systems may be compromised, so the communication plan uses channels outside the affected environment.

| Audience | Primary Channel | Fallback | Owner | Confirmation Method |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Incident command team | Dedicated bridge and separate-tenant secure messaging | Phone tree using personal mobiles from offline sheet | Incident Commander | Roll call at each checkpoint; absentees called within five minutes |
| Employees | External bulk SMS | External status page, manager phone cascade, and site notices | Communications Lead with HR | Staff reply “1”; target 90% acknowledgement by 10:30 |
| Executives and Board | Direct call from Incident Commander or CEO office | SMS and personal email through Company Secretary | Incident Commander and Company Secretary | Verbal acknowledgement logged with time |
| Customers and B2B clients | Account-manager call and unaffected email domain | Status page and SMS | Customer Lead | Call log, reply, or read receipt |
| Bank and payroll partners | Verified relationship-manager call | Secure bank portal or in-person visit | Finance and Payroll Lead | Call-back, reference number, and written out-of-band confirmation |
| Legal Counsel and regulators | Counsel's secure line and portal | External encrypted email | General Counsel | Contact time and reference recorded |

### 12.1 Readiness Checks

- Confirm that the SMS provider works without corporate credentials.
- Confirm that the external status page remains accessible.
- Maintain offline contact lists.
- Use a pre-agreed code word for identity validation.
- Test the phone tree regularly.
- Record the time, owner, audience, content, approval, and acknowledgement of every message.

The organisation should use a reactive holding statement only after approval:

## 15. Ethical and Legal Considerations

- Communicate uncertainty honestly.
- Do not use reassurance as a substitute for evidence.
- Avoid naming individuals or attributing blame prematurely.
- Protect confidential, personal, and privileged information.
- Preserve evidence connected to the screenshot and incident.
- Let General Counsel determine legal notification duties.
- Use approved channels for law enforcement, regulators, and insurers.
- Avoid direct attacker engagement outside authorised command and Legal processes.
- Ensure accessibility for employees and customers who cannot use the primary digital channel.

## 16. Critical Reflection

The most difficult communication issue was payroll. Employees need reassurance, but saying “payroll will be fine” would create an unsupported promise. The systems may be affected, bank cut-offs remain unconfirmed, and a failed promise on Friday could cause greater damage than an honest statement on Monday.

The selected approach was to:

- Identify payroll as a priority
- State that timing remained unconfirmed
- Avoid positive or negative speculation
- Commit to a specific update at 12:30
- Provide practical actions that employees could take immediately

The first message was held until after the alignment call and Legal review. This created a short delay, but one clean and approved message was judged safer than releasing conflicting statements and corrections. The approach recognises that every message may later be examined by employees, customers, regulators, counsel, the media, or an attacker.

## 17. Key Decisions and Trade-Offs

| Decision | Benefit | Trade-Off or Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Use “possible cyber incident” | Accurate and defensible | May appear less decisive than stronger language |
| Delay first message for alignment and Legal review | Reduces contradiction and legal exposure | Allows a short period of uncertainty |
| Address screenshot without validating it | Reduces spread and speculation | Repetition may increase awareness of it |
| Avoid payroll promise | Protects credibility and accuracy | Employees may feel insufficiently reassured |
| Use fixed update times | Builds trust and reduces rumours | Requires updates even when facts do not change |
| Treat corporate channels as untrusted | Improves resilience | Requires maintenance and testing of alternative channels |
| Restrict spokespersons | Creates message consistency | May slow responses if deputies are not prepared |

## 18. Recommendations

1. Maintain an offline, role-based crisis contact directory.
2. Test the bulk-SMS platform and external status page quarterly.
3. Conduct twice-yearly crisis communication exercises.
4. Maintain pre-approved employee, customer, media, regulator, and payroll templates.
5. Define primary and deputy spokespeople for each stakeholder group.
6. Establish a protected fact log and communication decision log.
7. Agree bank payroll cut-offs and contingency procedures before an incident.
8. Define legal and regulatory notification workflows by jurisdiction.
9. Train staff not to forward screenshots, rumours, or suspected attacker communications.
10. Monitor acknowledgement rates and contact non-responders through manager cascades.
11. Review cyber-insurance notification requirements and authorised response providers.
12. Include phishing and fraudulent-payment warnings in external incident messages.

## 20. Conclusion

The report demonstrates that effective crisis communication depends on disciplined governance rather than rapid messaging alone. The organisation must identify what is verified, assign owners to unresolved questions, control who may speak, use resilient channels, tailor messages to each stakeholder group, and issue updates at predictable times.

The strongest feature of the response is its refusal to overstate the evidence. The report does not confirm ransomware, a breach, customer-data exposure, an attacker, or payroll failure. Instead, it provides accurate operational information, clear actions, named decision points, and a structured route for future updates.

By combining message discipline, stakeholder prioritisation, legal review, out-of-band channels, escalation triggers, and transparent uncertainty, the plan protects trust while allowing the technical investigation and operational response to continue.
